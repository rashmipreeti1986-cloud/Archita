01 / Know the risks

How could someone become a victim of cybercrime?

Cybercrime can affect anyone. It often begins with a small mistake, a convincing message or information shared without checking.

🎣

Phishing messages

A fake email, text or social-media message may pretend to come from a trusted service and trick someone into sharing login or payment details.

Watch for urgent links
🔓

Weak or reused passwords

Short, predictable or repeated passwords can make several accounts vulnerable if one password is exposed.

Protect every account
🎭

Impersonation scams

A scammer may pretend to be a friend, teacher, company or official to create trust and request money or private information.

Verify who is asking
🪪

Identity theft

Personal details or account access may be misused to impersonate someone, open accounts or deceive other people.

Keep personal data private
🦠

Malware and ransomware

Harmful software can disrupt devices, steal information or lock access to files. Suspicious downloads and attachments can be risky.

Download carefully
💬

Cyberbullying and harassment

Repeated hurtful messages, threats or sharing someone's private content without permission can cause real harm.

Block, save evidence, report
02 / Lock it down

Build a stronger password.

A good password should be long, difficult to guess and different for every account. A password manager can help you keep track of unique passwords.

✓
Make it longAim for at least 14 characters; a longer passphrase can be easier to remember.
✓
Mix character typesUse a combination of uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers and symbols where supported.
✓
Keep it uniqueUse a different password for each important account.
✓
Avoid personal cluesDo not use your name, birthday, school, phone number or obvious sequences.
✓
Turn on multi-factor authenticationMFA adds another verification step, so a password alone is less likely to be enough.

03 / Cyber awareness corner

Pause. Check. Protect. Report.

Build these small habits into your everyday digital life.

01 — PAUSE

Don't rush

Be careful with messages that create panic, pressure you to act quickly or promise unbelievable rewards.

02 — CHECK

Verify the source

Check the full website address and contact the person or organisation through a trusted channel.

03 — PROTECT

Secure your accounts

Use unique passwords, enable MFA, update devices and limit what you share publicly.

04 — REPORT

Speak up

Save relevant evidence, block suspicious accounts and report harmful activity to the platform or appropriate authority.

⚠️
Remember: Never share an OTP, PIN or password.

A genuine bank or support representative should not ask you to reveal these secret credentials. Ask a trusted adult for help if a message or request feels suspicious.

04 / Did you know?

Cybercrime facts worth remembering.

Knowing how digital threats work makes it easier to recognise warning signs.

01

Phishing is about deception. A message or website can imitate a trusted brand or person to trick someone into revealing information.

02

Ransomware targets access to data. It can lock or encrypt files and demand payment. Backups and updated software are important protections.

03

MFA adds another layer. It asks for an additional proof of identity beyond a password, helping protect accounts.

04

Scams can use AI. Fake websites, messages, voices or videos may look convincing. Verify unusual requests through a separate trusted channel.

05

Cybercrime is broader than hacking. It can include online financial fraud, impersonation, identity misuse, harassment and harmful software.

06

Your digital footprint matters. Photos, location clues and personal details shared online can reveal more than you intend.
